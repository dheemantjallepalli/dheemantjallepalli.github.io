02 — Research Experience

Where I've Worked

  1. Mar 2026 — Now

    Harvard University · Bionics Lab

    Research Fellow · Prof. Shriya S. Srinivasan · Cambridge, MA

    • Ran a multi-day surface EMG biofeedback study showing people can learn to fire two synergistic plantar-flexor muscles independently — turning one degree of freedom into multi-DOF control
    • Built a portable multi-channel sEMG system (custom analog front-end, filtering, embedded firmware) now used as the at-home platform for a study in lower-limb amputation
    • Engineered a handheld myoelectric controller that emulates a keyboard, giving up to 4 degrees of freedom in unmodified commercial games
    • Built closed-loop functional electrical stimulation driven by muscle-state-aware deep learning controllers, benchmarked against PID on force tracking and fatigue
  2. Aug 2024 — Mar 2026

    Carnegie Mellon University · NeuroMechatronics Lab

    Research Engineer · Prof. Douglas J. Weber · Pittsburgh, PA

    • Decoded 8-channel surface EMG into continuous wrist and finger movement for real-time games (up to 92% gesture accuracy) and drove a custom 3D-printed multi-DOF robotic arm
    • Showed proportional velocity control beats position-based and discrete gesture decoding for natural game control — the basis of my MS thesis
    • Built a Python framework tracking motor recovery under spinal cord stimulation in stroke, using electrodermal and heart-rate-variability biomarkers
    • Built a closed-loop stimulation interface on CorTec’s implantable device API, and HD-EMG motor unit decomposition tools
  3. Jan 2025 — Dec 2025

    Carnegie Mellon University · Wood Lab

    Research Assistant · Prof. Sossena Wood · Pittsburgh, PA

    • Measured cerebral and forearm blood flow in adults with sickle cell disease, a population prior pediatric studies left out

    • Presented at the 67th American Society of Hematology Annual Meeting (ASH 2025)
  4. Jan 2025 — May 2025

04 — About Me & Contact

I decode movement from muscle.

I'm a Research Fellow at the Harvard Bionics Lab, working where neural engineering, wearable biosensing and human–machine interfaces meet. I decode neuromotor intent from surface EMG to drive neuroprosthetics, exoskeletons and interactive rehabilitation, with the goal of restoring motor function after neurological injury.

I'm also interested in closed-loop neurorehabilitation, pairing biosignal decoding with neuromodulation and spinal cord stimulation to promote recovery. Away from the bench: football (I captained my school team), middle-distance running, and too many photos of light installations.
