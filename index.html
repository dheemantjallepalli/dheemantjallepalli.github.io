01 — About Me

Restoring Movement Through Neural Engineering

I am a Research Fellow at the Harvard Bionics Lab, developing human–machine interfaces that restore and augment motor function after neurological injury and limb loss.

My work centres on decoding neuromotor intent from surface and intramuscular EMG and translating it into intuitive, real-time control of neuroprosthetics, exoskeletons and interactive rehabilitation systems — from custom biosignal hardware and embedded firmware to machine-learning decoders and closed-loop functional electrical stimulation.

Previously, as a Research Engineer in Prof. Douglas Weber's NeuroMechatronics Lab at Carnegie Mellon University, I built EMG-driven neuromotor interfaces for gamified rehabilitation and studied motor recovery under spinal cord stimulation. My earlier research spans computational pathology at Mila, resting-state fMRI connectivity at IIT Jodhpur, and vision and gait at the University of Manitoba.

My long-term goal is closed-loop neurotechnology that pairs biosignal decoding with neuromodulation to drive lasting, personalised motor recovery.
